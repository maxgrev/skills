# First two lines and preview

Read this only when the opening or preview is in scope. On X Articles, the preview comes from the article body. Write usable opening lines that begin delivering the headline's promise.

## Write against the chosen headline

Keep the headline fixed for this pass. Find one useful fact it has not already said: scope, method, result, example, limitation, or a concrete detail that begins the story.

Prefer a short complete first sentence followed by a longer sentence when that rhythm suits the voice. The source's six-word-or-fewer first sentences had a small association with reaching 100k; do not cut a clear sentence into a fragment to meet six words.

| Headline form | Opening that can serve it |
| --- | --- |
| How-to | Start with the task, common obstacle, or first useful step |
| Guide or resource | Show what is inside and how the reader can use it |
| First-person result | State a supported result or condition, then begin explaining what happened |
| Announcement | Say what the thing does and the detail that matters to the reader |
| Argument or story | Start with the evidence, event, or scene that supports the claim |

These pairings are craft suggestions. They are not individually proven performance effects.

## Check the pair

- The preview adds information rather than paraphrasing the title.
- Its first sentence is a real sentence, with no greeting, byline, or generic setup.
- The first visible part names something concrete and gives the reader value before any cutoff.
- The voice fits the headline; preserve ordinary capitalization and proper names.
- A rhetorical question does not delay the point. Use a question only when it has a real job.
- Every number, personal event, and credential comes from the supplied facts.
- The opening continues naturally into the body, without a forced cliffhanger.

The source describes about 130 visible characters on the X card it observed in September 2026. Treat that as a rough preview check, not a current platform limit. Follow any limit supplied by the user or observed in the current channel. Read the first 130 characters as a quick truncation test; do not force a sentence to break there or promise that a particular device will show exactly that amount.

## Transfer example

Given the Notion tutorial in [patterns.md](patterns.md):

> Plan a month of portfolio posts in Notion (full template)
>
> Start with your last three projects.
> The template turns each one into a case study, a process post, and a short lesson, then schedules them across the month.

Use this opening only if the actual template works that way. Without that detail, read the resource before writing it or choose an opening the brief supports.

Return the headline and two lines together. Generate extra opening options only when requested or when the user needs to choose between distinct approaches.
