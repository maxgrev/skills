# Headline eval

Run every applicable check before returning finished work. Fix a failed check, then review the revised title. Keep this pass internal unless the user asks for an audit or needs to understand a material tradeoff.

## Content and truth

- Can you point to the draft or brief that supports each claim, number, timeframe, named tool, and first-person result? Remove or qualify anything you cannot support.
- Does the article deliver the exact result, finding, story, or resource the headline promises? A related discussion does not count as delivery.
- Does a claimed speed describe the author's observed experience or a supported path for the reader? Do not turn one person's result into a general guarantee.
- Does "full," "complete," "exact template," or an equivalent describe what the reader actually receives within the stated scope? Remove the tag if key steps, files, or conditions are missing.
- Is an introduction truly new in the context of this piece? Remove announcement wording from an ordinary review or retrospective.

## Reader and clarity

- Can the intended reader tell what the piece is about and why it is worth opening without seeing your explanation?
- Does the title express a useful outcome or a clear editorial reason to read? Naming a broad topic alone rarely supplies enough value.
- Do named tools, people, and companies belong to the content and help this audience? Remove unrelated trend names.
- Does any withheld detail create useful curiosity while preserving the subject and value? Reveal enough if the title is vague.
- Would the title still fit many unrelated articles? Replace generic praise with a supported distinction.

## Language and fit

- Does it sound like the user's voice? Remove copied cadence, default title case, forced lowercase, and inflated words that do not belong.
- Does each word add meaning? Cut filler without deleting a useful condition or specific.
- Are the verbs concrete and the title easy to say aloud? Fix tangled syntax and abstract labels.
- Does punctuation have a job? Remove default colon reveals, decorative dashes, fragment stacks, and unsupported contrast setups.
- Does the headline meet the user's channel, length, format, and count constraints? Shorten only where needed for clarity or those constraints.

## Pattern check for X

- Did you read the move definitions and count each family only once?
- Did you inspect failure marks separately from the level? L4 does not mean mark-free.
- Did you check regex hits against their actual meaning? A product name containing "Why" or a mythological Hermes is not an editorial defect or an AI-tool promise merely because it matches.
- Did you preserve exact figures and relevant names instead of changing facts to gain a move?
- Are levels presented as pattern counts rather than quality ratings or reach predictions?
- If you use examples, are they from a relevant topic, and did you consider smaller-account examples when available? Do not mistake the frozen CSV for current trend data.

## Variants, preview, and delivery

- Do alternatives change the angle or leading value? Replace near-duplicate adjective swaps.
- Is the recommendation based first on truthful reader value, content fit, and clarity? Reconsider a choice made only because it has more moves.
- If a preview is included, does it add a supported fact and begin delivering the promise without a forced cutoff?
- Did you return the requested headline work first, with only necessary explanation? Remove an unwanted scoring table, extra rewrite, or approval step.
