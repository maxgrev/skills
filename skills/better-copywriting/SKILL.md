# Better Copywriting

Write truthful copy that moves the right prospect toward a clear action. Optimize for qualified response and customer fit, not attention at any cost.

## Choose the job

- **Create:** Build copy from product, audience, offer, and channel facts.
- **Rewrite:** Preserve verified claims, offer terms, brand voice, and intent while improving strategy and execution.
- **Humanize:** Remove AI-telltale wording, rhythm, structure, and formatting while preserving the sales argument, evidence, offer, and genuine brand voice.
- **Critique:** Diagnose the highest-leverage failures. Do not rewrite unless the user asks.
- **Generate variants:** Explore materially different promises, mechanisms, awareness levels, or emotional angles. Do not return synonyms disguised as tests.

If the user wants ordinary prose editing without a commercial or campaign action, this is not the right skill.

## Establish the brief

Extract these inputs from the request and supplied material:

- desired action and destination;
- audience, situation, and traffic source;
- product or service facts;
- offer, price, terms, and availability;
- proof the user can substantiate;
- channel, format, length, and platform constraints;
- brand voice, voice samples, forbidden phrases, and claims that must remain unchanged.

Ask only for information whose absence would materially change the result. Otherwise make a conservative assumption, state it briefly, and proceed. Never invent a statistic, testimonial, customer result, certification, limitation, price, deadline, guarantee, or product capability. Use a clear bracketed placeholder only when the missing fact is essential to usable copy.

## Learn from the source examples

The source library is primary calibration, not decoration. Preserve the distinction between:

- **verbatim exemplar:** exact historical wording retained for study;
- **documented case:** a campaign or test summarized from the source record;
- **transfer example:** new copy written to demonstrate the principle.

Study the exact exemplar before abstracting the device. Notice its promise, specificity, cadence, syntax, information order, proof, imagery, and implied prospect. Then transfer the underlying decision to the user's facts. Do not silently paraphrase an exemplar and call it historical, and do not copy a distinctive line with nouns swapped.

When the source wording contains obsolete, discriminatory, sensational, medical, financial, or otherwise unsupported claims, treat it as evidence of structure—not permission to reuse the claim or posture. Current truth, fit, platform rules, and user-supplied substantiation always govern the delivered copy.

## Build the copy

1. Form an internal brief: prospect, dominant desire, awareness, market sophistication, problem, desired outcome, mechanism, proof, offer, objections, source, and action. Do not expose this brief unless it helps the user evaluate a material assumption.
2. Choose one primary promise and one organizing idea. Secondary benefits must reinforce it rather than compete with it.
3. Match the entrance to what the prospect already knows. Do not explain a familiar product to a most-aware buyer or make a cold, unaware prospect absorb a premature product pitch.
4. Connect the promise to a credible mechanism or reason why. Place proof near the claim it supports.
5. Write the smallest complete argument the sale requires. Make each section earn the next one.
6. Present the offer and next action without forcing the reader to reconstruct terms.
7. Run the human-writing pass. Remove default AI wording, symmetry, filler, and formatting while preserving deliberate sales devices and the user's voice.
8. Run the eval, revise, read the copy aloud, and return the copy first.

## Non-negotiables

- Attract the right buyer, not the largest number of clicks.
- Preserve the user's product, offer, audience, and channel choices unless asked to challenge them.
- Lead with buyer value, but retain facts needed for an informed decision.
- Prefer concrete outcomes, active verbs, exact mechanisms, and sensory detail to abstractions and superlatives.
- Preserve useful irregularity in the user's voice. Do not make every sentence, paragraph, benefit, or section equally polished or structurally identical.
- Treat rhetorical devices as choices, not defaults. Repetition, fragments, contrast, lists, and strong punctuation must perform a specific selling job.
- Use curiosity only when the copy closes the loop.
- Use emotion to create relevance and logic to support the decision.
- Treat objections directly; do not conceal material limitations.
- Use urgency and scarcity only when they are real and relevant.
- Make guarantees precise about scope, conditions, timing, and remedy.
- Keep the ad, landing page, checkout, and delivered product consistent.
- Do not turn historical health, financial, or performance claims into modern copy without current substantiation and appropriate qualification.
- Do not reveal framework names or cite famous copywriters in the customer-facing copy unless the user asks.

## Deliver the result

For creation or rewriting, lead with finished copy. Add only what helps the user use or judge it: essential assumptions, a small set of genuinely different alternatives, or a concise explanation of a consequential strategic choice.

For critiques, prioritize findings by expected effect on response: audience and offer first, then promise and proof, then structure and language. Quote the affected copy and give a concrete fix. Distinguish a strategy problem from a sentence problem.
