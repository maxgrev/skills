# Copywriting eval

Run every applicable check before delivering created or rewritten copy. Fix failures rather than merely reporting them. For critique-only work, use the failures to structure the report and separate strategy from execution.

## Audience and objective

- [ ] The intended prospect and buying situation are identifiable.
- [ ] The copy asks for one clear primary action appropriate to this stage.
- [ ] The traffic source and temperature are reflected in the opening and amount of explanation.
- [ ] The ad or email promise matches the destination and delivered product.
- [ ] The copy seeks qualified response, not indiscriminate attention.

## Desire, awareness, and sophistication

- [ ] One dominant desire organizes the message.
- [ ] The opening matches what the prospect already knows.
- [ ] The claim and mechanism are credible for the market's sophistication.
- [ ] Secondary benefits reinforce rather than compete with the primary promise.
- [ ] The product has a useful role in the idea; the concept is not detachable cleverness.

## Offer and economics

- [ ] The reader can tell what they receive, what it costs, and what to do next.
- [ ] Billing, delivery, eligibility, and material limitations are clear where relevant.
- [ ] Bonuses remove an obstacle or accelerate the result rather than pad a value stack.
- [ ] Price anchors and comparisons are genuine and fairly framed.
- [ ] Scarcity, deadlines, and capacity limits are real.
- [ ] The guarantee states scope, conditions, timing, and remedy.

## Claims and proof

- [ ] No fact, result, statistic, testimonial, certification, price, deadline, or capability was invented.
- [ ] Objective claims can be substantiated under the conditions implied by the copy.
- [ ] Proof appears close to the claim it supports.
- [ ] Exceptional customer results are not presented as typical without support.
- [ ] Comparisons disclose the basis or conditions that could change the conclusion.
- [ ] Health, financial, safety, or regulated claims receive appropriate caution and qualification.
- [ ] Material drawbacks are not concealed by framing or omission.

## Headline and opening

- [ ] The intended prospect can recognize relevance quickly.
- [ ] The headline leads with a valuable, supportable idea rather than generic excitement.
- [ ] The headline and visual communicate one coherent hook.
- [ ] Curiosity is relevant and paid off.
- [ ] The first sentence is easy to enter and continues the headline's thought.
- [ ] The opening contains no company-centered throat clearing.

## Structure and momentum

- [ ] Each section performs a distinct selling job.
- [ ] Questions raised by the copy are answered before the offer depends on them.
- [ ] The mechanism connects product features to the promised outcome.
- [ ] The strongest objection receives a direct, credible answer.
- [ ] Proof, offer, risk reversal, and CTA arrive when the reader needs them.
- [ ] Long copy adds belief or desire; short copy does not omit essential decision information.

## Language and voice

- [ ] Concrete outcomes, active verbs, and complete mental pictures replace avoidable abstractions.
- [ ] Specific numbers and details are verified, not decorative precision.
- [ ] Sentences complete one manageable thought and link cleanly to the next.
- [ ] Paragraphs have a clear core idea and readable rhythm.
- [ ] Emotional language fits the product and does not manufacture panic, shame, or false hope.
- [ ] Jargon remains only when the audience uses it or the detail affects the decision.
- [ ] The user's established voice survives the edit.
- [ ] The copy does not sound like a collage of swipe-file formulas.
- [ ] Source examples informed strategic decisions without donating distinctive phrases or syntax too closely.

## Human voice and AI tells

- [ ] The draft preserves the user's vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, warmth, humor, restraint, and level of polish where supplied.
- [ ] No sentence could be pasted unchanged onto a competitor's page because it contains only generic praise or feeling.
- [ ] Flagged AI words, empty transitions, and unsupported copywriting superlatives from [human-writing.md](human-writing.md) are removed or justified by voice and meaning.
- [ ] Generic binary contrasts, negative listings, faux-insight setups, and stock entrances are gone.
- [ ] Colons introduce real lists, labels, examples, quotations, or offer inventories rather than dramatic reveals.
- [ ] Benefits, sections, bullets, adjectives, and fragments use their natural count rather than default groups of three.
- [ ] No false “from X to Y” range turns unrelated items into a fake journey.
- [ ] No trailing “highlighting,” “underscoring,” “showcasing,” or “reflecting” clause substitutes for a real consequence.
- [ ] Authority and popularity claims name a source; “experts agree,” “studies show,” and similar attributions are not invented.
- [ ] The draft repeats the clearest product and audience terms instead of cycling through synonyms.
- [ ] Punchy fragments, questions, repetition, punctuation, and contrast each perform a deliberate selling job rather than supply generic drama.
- [ ] Sentence lengths, paragraph shapes, and section structures do not repeat with robotic symmetry.
- [ ] Formatting serves scanning and decision-making without emoji headings, decorative bold, needless micro-sections, or repetitive inline labels.
- [ ] Short copy has no em dash; longer copy uses at most one or two unless the supplied voice clearly calls for more.
- [ ] The ending lands on a concrete offer, proof point, instruction, or next action rather than a recap, aphorism, or manifesto line.
- [ ] Customer-facing copy contains no chatbot residue.

## Source fidelity

- [ ] Any historical wording presented to the user as an example is actually verbatim and attributed; summaries and transfer examples are labeled.
- [ ] The copywriter studied the relevant source reference instead of relying only on a remembered slogan or abstract rule.
- [ ] The transfer preserves the useful decision—awareness entrance, promise, mechanism, proof, sequence, or offer—without performing a noun-swap imitation.
- [ ] Historical claims, scarcity, stereotypes, and pressure devices were not treated as current permission.
- [ ] The source calibration improved the user's message rather than replacing their voice with a famous copywriter's persona.

## CTA and use

- [ ] The CTA names a concrete action and sets an accurate expectation for what follows.
- [ ] The path to respond is simple and contains no contradictory instructions.
- [ ] Repeated CTAs appear only after the copy has earned another decision point.
- [ ] Platform length, format, disclosure, and placement constraints are satisfied.
- [ ] Placeholders and assumptions are visible and cannot be mistaken for final facts.

## Final challenge

Before delivery, answer these questions internally:

1. What is the strongest reason the intended prospect would still say no?
2. Is that a copy problem, an offer problem, a proof problem, or a product/traffic problem?
3. Which sentence makes the largest promise, and is the adjacent support strong enough?
4. What can be cut without reducing desire, belief, clarity, or action?
5. What sounds like advertising instead of a knowledgeable person explaining a worthwhile decision?
6. What still makes this read as AI-generated, and which exact words, rhythms, structures, or formatting choices create that impression?

Revise once more based on the answers. If a material gap requires user evidence or a business decision, disclose it concisely after the copy.
