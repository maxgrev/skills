# Headlines, hooks, and subject lines

Read this reference for headlines, subject lines, ad hooks, leads, captions, short ads, or the first screen of a landing page. Before substantial headline generation, use [source-index.md](source-index.md) to load at least one relevant source library. Ogilvy and Schwartz are the default pair for product-led headlines; Whitman and Hopkins are the default pair for direct-response offers and tests.

## The headline's job

A headline selects the right prospect, makes a relevant promise or opens a useful question, and earns the next line. It does not need to complete the sale.

Before writing, know:

- the prospect's awareness and traffic source;
- the dominant desire or tension;
- the strongest supportable promise;
- the market's familiarity with similar claims;
- the mechanism, proof, and offer available below;
- the space and platform constraints.

Generate candidates across materially different angle families before polishing. A useful set changes what leads: outcome, problem, mechanism, proof, identity, story, offer, or objection. Do not make a list of cosmetic rewrites.

## Angle families and transfer examples

The examples below are **transfer examples**, not historical controls. Use them only after studying relevant verbatim exemplars. Adapt the decision, not either set of wording.

### Direct outcome

State the desired result when the market is ready to believe it.

> Close the Books Before Friday

### Offer

Use for most-aware prospects when terms are the news.

> Add Unlimited Guests at No Extra Cost Through June 30

### Problem crystallization

Name a costly problem the prospect recognizes but has not fully explained.

> Your Churn Starts Two Weeks Before the Cancellation Email

### Mechanism

Introduce the reason the result is possible.

> The Contract-First Invoice Check That Stops Duplicate Payments

### Specific proof

Lead with a verified measurement, demonstration, or representative result.

> See How the Team Cut Review Time From 42 Minutes to 11

### How-to

Promise useful instruction, not a disguised pitch.

> How to Find the Three Checkout Fields Customers Keep Abandoning

### News or change

Use when something is genuinely new, improved, released, or newly available.

> Now Every Plan Includes Same-Day Exports

### Demonstration

Invite the prospect to see the product perform.

> Upload One Invoice and Watch the Errors Light Up

### Story

Open a loop around a relevant person, decision, mistake, or discovery.

> Nina Almost Rejected the Best-Performing Ad

### Identification

Call out the situation or identity that makes the product relevant.

> For Light Sleepers Who Feel Every Degree and Shift

### Objection reversal

Name the resistance and give the reader a reason to reconsider.

> Yes, It Costs More. Here Is Why You Will Not Replace It Next Year.

### Comparison

Contrast alternatives under fair, disclosed conditions.

> What a $29 Charger Leaves Out That This One Includes

### Warning or risk

Use only for a real, material danger and connect it to useful action.

> Before You Approve the Batch, Check These Two Duplicate-Payment Signals

### Question

Ask only when the reader cares about the answer and the copy can supply it.

> Which of These Renewal Emails Is Quietly Increasing Refunds?

### Curiosity

Withhold a detail, not the relevance or value.

> We Removed the Product's Most Popular Feature. Support Tickets Fell.

### Scarcity or urgency

Name the real constraint rather than issuing a generic command.

> Twelve Installation Slots Remain Before the October Freeze

## Match awareness

- **Most aware:** Product or offer name plus terms, improvement, or deadline.
- **Product aware:** Difference, proof, new use, comparison, or objection.
- **Solution aware:** Desired result plus mechanism.
- **Problem aware:** Problem, symptom, consequence, or diagnostic insight.
- **Unaware:** Story, identity, observation, or news that leads naturally to the problem.

## Strengthen a sound idea

Once the central idea works, improve it through one useful dimension:

- replace an abstraction with a visible outcome;
- add a verified quantity, timeframe, or condition;
- name the prospect or situation;
- expose the mechanism;
- show the result in action;
- add relevant proof;
- remove a meaningful limitation;
- turn a generic claim into a fair comparison;
- make the offer terms explicit.

Shorter is better only when it retains the idea. Do not amputate specificity to create a slogan.

## Subject lines and platform hooks

Subject lines sell the open, not the entire offer. Maintain continuity between subject, preview text, first sentence, and CTA. Avoid reply bait, fake forwards, false urgency, or a personal-sounding subject that conceals a commercial message.

For interruption ads, the visual and first words form one hook. Do not write them independently. For search ads, mirror intent and differentiators rather than manufacturing mystery. For social video, make the first spoken line understandable without a caption and the first frame understandable without sound when the platform demands it.

## Common failures

- broad promise with no audience or condition;
- clever phrase that hides the product or value;
- unsupported superlative or speed claim;
- question whose answer is obviously no;
- curiosity that attracts people unlikely to buy;
- headline and landing page making different promises;
- mechanism name with no explanation;
- urgency with no real constraint;
- title-shaped throat clearing such as “The Future of Better Business”;
- stacking benefit, proof, urgency, novelty, and qualification until nothing leads.

## Headline check

Before choosing a control, ask:

- Will the intended prospect recognize that this is for them?
- Does it enter at the right awareness level?
- Is the leading idea valuable and supportable?
- Is it distinct enough for this market?
- Does it create the right expectation for the next line and destination?
- Could a competitor paste it above their product unchanged? If so, make it more specific.
