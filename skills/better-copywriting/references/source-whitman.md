# Drew Eric Whitman — *Ca$hvertising*

Read this reference for primary desires, benefit concentration, direct-response headline entrances, sensory language, risk reversal, social proof, and action friction.

## Provenance and use

Whitman's book is a practical catalog of psychological and response devices rather than an archive of famous controls. The 22 lines below are **verbatim short-form book examples** of his “psychologically potent headline starters,” checked against a [detailed transcription](https://auresnotes.com/summary-cashvertising-drew-eric-whitman/2/). The book's scope is confirmed by the [publisher description](https://www.simonandschuster.co.uk/books/Cashvertising/Drew-Eric-Whitman/Cashvertising-Series/9781601630322).

Treat them as demonstrations of entrances, not fill-in-the-blank copy. Several use exaggerated, fear-heavy, or health-oriented claims that require stronger evidence and better judgment in modern work.

## 22 verbatim headline examples

1. **FREE**

   > Free Book Shows You How to Write Sneaky Advertising That Practically Forces People to Send You Money!

   Price, mechanism, and a strong desired action appear immediately.

2. **NEW**

   > Powerful New Seminar Teaches Flea Marketers the Power of “Flea-Psych” to Drive People Into a Buying Frenzy

   Newness is attached to a named mechanism and a narrow audience.

3. **AT LAST**

   > At Last…A Bakery That Uses Only Organic Sugar, Flour, Milk, and Eggs!

   “At last” implies a long-standing unmet standard; the ingredient list proves the distinction.

4. **THIS**

   > This New Invention Stops Any Attacker in His Tracks Without a Gun, Knife, or Black Belt in Karate.

   The result is combined with removal of three expected requirements.

5. **ANNOUNCING**

   > Announcing the Hottest New Sandwich Craze From Southern California: The Malibu Crust Pocket!

   Announcement, social momentum, place, and a named product create launch energy.

6. **WARNING**

   > WARNING! Some Dog Groomers Wrap a Noose Around Your Dog’s Neck!

   A vivid danger selects protective owners. Modern use requires proportionate, substantiated risk.

7. **JUST RELEASED**

   > Just Released: Psychologist’s Study Reveals Little-Known Speaking Patterns That Immediately Put Rude Salespeople in Their Place.

   Timeliness, authority, hidden knowledge, recognizable antagonist, and immediate result combine.

8. **NOW**

   > Now You Can Stop Any Attacker Without Guns, Knives, or a Black Belt in Karate.

   “Now” makes the capability newly available; the rest removes expected barriers.

9. **HERE’S**

   > Here’s How a 95-Pound Granny Made a 275-Pound Psychopathic Killer Cry Like a Baby for its Rattle….

   Extreme contrast, numbers, character, and a reversed power relationship generate story pressure.

10. **THESE**

    > These Three Very Italian Men Make a Pizza to Kill For.

    People and provenance give a familiar product a character-led story.

11. **WHICH OF**

    > Which of These Hot Bodies Would YOU Like to Show Off?

    The reader is made to choose and imagine possession. Avoid objectifying applications.

12. **FINALLY**

    > Finally…a Self-Improvement Seminar That Moves, Empowers, and Transforms You for Life!

    The opening implies category disappointment before offering a complete alternative.

13. **LOOK**

    > LOOK! Now You Can Buy Cotton Candy Machines at Wholesale Prices.

    A visual command precedes news, product, and price advantage.

14. **PRESENTING**

    > Presenting the Easiest Way Ever Developed to Learn the Piano.

    Formal unveiling is attached to the category's dominant effort objection.

15. **INTRODUCING**

    > Introducing the Only Water Ice Stand in Philly that Uses Real Fresh Fruit.

    Introduction, exclusivity, locality, and ingredient proof appear in one line.

16. **HOW**

    > How to Sing Like an American Idol in 90 Days or Less—Guaranteed.

    Desired identity, timeframe, and risk reversal are concentrated.

17. **AMAZING**

    > Amazing New DVD Lowers Your Blood Pressure by Just Watching It!

    The easy mechanism is the surprise. Do not transfer the medical claim without appropriate evidence and review.

18. **DO YOU**

    > Do You Know How to Stop Vicious Dog Attacks with the Push of a Button?

    The question exposes a knowledge gap and promises a simple control action.

19. **WOULD YOU**

    > Would You Trade $2 for Our Famous Brick-Oven Pizza?

    Payment is reframed as an obviously favorable exchange.

20. **CAN YOU**

    > Can You Be Sure Your Child Won’t Get Kidnapped?

    The impossibility of certainty intensifies parental fear. Use risk framing ethically and never manufacture danger.

21. **IF YOU**

    > If You Hate Cleaning Your Pool, This Ad Brings Good News!

    A disliked task qualifies the prospect, while “good news” opens the solution.

22. **STARTING TODAY**

    > Starting Today You Can Dance 97% Better…If You Follow These Rules.

    Immediate availability, an exact improvement, and a condition create a testable-looking claim; real use needs a defensible measurement.

## 20 working lessons

1. **Optimize for response.** The ad exists to change behavior, not collect compliments.
2. **Begin with the prospect.** Their situation and desire matter more than company history.
3. **Connect to a primary human desire.** Survival, pleasure, safety, intimacy, comfort, status, care for loved ones, and approval are recurring roots.
4. **Choose one dominant desire.** Too many motives weaken recognition and force.
5. **Translate features into outcomes.** Ask what the feature lets the buyer do, feel, avoid, protect, or become.
6. **Put the strongest benefit in the headline.** Do not hide the central reason to continue.
7. **Make the headline stop and pull.** It should select the right person and motivate the next line.
8. **Use self-interest, news, curiosity, or ease.** Strong openings often combine two, but clarity remains primary.
9. **Write for immediate comprehension.** Short words, concrete sentences, and clean paragraphs reduce effort.
10. **Use defensible specificity.** Exact times, quantities, conditions, and mechanisms outperform superlatives when true.
11. **Direct a mental movie.** Let the reader see, hear, feel, smell, or taste the result.
12. **Show the product in use.** Demonstrated outcomes are stronger than isolated product shots.
13. **Match desired identity.** People choose products that reinforce who they are or want to become.
14. **Supply proof.** Demonstration, research, contextual testimony, comparison, and verified figures create belief.
15. **Use social proof and authority for relevance, not decoration.**
16. **Fight inertia.** Explain the next step, remove friction, and ask for it.
17. **Reverse reasonable risk.** A clear guarantee moves uncertainty toward the seller.
18. **Use urgency only when real.** Inventory, capacity, and deadlines need an honest reason.
19. **Let complexity determine length.** Complete the sale without padding.
20. **Test response, not taste.** Judge headlines, proof, offers, creative, and CTAs by sales or qualified conversion.

## Transfer questions

- Which opening word is genuinely true here: new, now, finally, free, warning, how, or none?
- Is the biggest desire in the headline, or did a clever device replace it?
- Can the benefit be experienced as a mental movie?
- What friction makes the prospect postpone action?
- What evidence would make the specificity believable rather than theatrical?
